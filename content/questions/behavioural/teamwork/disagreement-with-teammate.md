## Question
Tell me about a time you disagreed with a teammate on a technical decision. How did you handle it?

## Answer
Answer with **STAR**: Situation, Task, Action, Result.

- **Situation**: one or two sentences of context: the project, the team, and
  what was at stake.
- **Task**: the decision in question and why it mattered, such as choosing
  between two designs under a deadline.
- **Action**: what *you* did. You understood their reasoning before arguing
  yours, framed the discussion around requirements rather than preferences,
  gathered evidence (a benchmark, a prototype, production data), and proposed a
  fair way to decide. Once the decision was made, you committed to it fully,
  even if it wasn't your idea.
- **Result**: the outcome, measured if possible, and what you learned about
  working through disagreement.

Interviewers are listening for respect, evidence over ego, and the ability to
disagree and then commit.

## Key points
- structure the story with STAR
- show you understood the other view first
- decide with evidence, not seniority or volume
- disagree and commit
- finish with a measurable result and a lesson

## Explanation
This is a template, not an answer to memorise. Replace it with a real story from
your own projects; interviewers quickly spot a generic, rehearsed answer.
